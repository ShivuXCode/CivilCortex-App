FEDERAL HIGHWAY ADMINISTRATION (FHWA)
BRIDGE INSPECTION AND MAINTENANCE GUIDELINES
Section 4: Concrete Deck and Substructure Defects

4.1 SPALLING AND DELAMINATION
Spalling refers to the detachment of fragments of concrete from the surface, often exposing reinforcing steel (rebar). 
- Routine Priority (Low Risk): Minor spalling (< 1 inch deep) with no exposed rebar. Monitor and schedule routine spalling repair with standard repair mortar.
- Urgent Priority (Medium Risk): Spalling exposing rebar with signs of mild corrosion. Requires spalling repair using anti-corrosion coating and structural repair mortar within the next maintenance cycle.
- Immediate Priority (High Risk): Extensive spalling on load-bearing elements (e.g., piers, columns) with severe rebar corrosion or section loss. Requires immediate shoring and concrete reinforcement or foundation underpinning.

4.2 STRUCTURAL CRACKING
Structural cracks indicate potential load-bearing distress.
- Cracks wider than 0.05 inches (1.3 mm) in load-bearing substructures must be addressed via epoxy injection to restore structural integrity.
- If cracking is accompanied by active settlement, foundation underpinning is mandated.

4.3 NON-STRUCTURAL CRACKING (SHRINKAGE/TEMPERATURE)
- Hairline cracks (< 0.01 inches) on bridge decks or retaining walls are typically non-structural. The standard maintenance action is surface sealing using polyurethane sealant to prevent water ingress and freeze-thaw damage.
